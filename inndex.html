Our Philosophy

We cook the way
the seasons ask us to.

Noir & Nectar began in a narrow Lisbon kitchen with two ovens, one marble slab and a stubborn belief: that food made slowly, in small quantities, by people who care, tastes like something else entirely.

A decade later we still work the same way. We visit every farm we buy from. We refuse to freeze. We let the dough rest as long as it needs. And we write the harvest date on every jar, because you deserve to know.

Nothing here is engineered for shelf life. Everything here is made for the moment it meets your table.

Of slow craft
How It's Made

Four steps.
No shortcuts.

From the field to your table, every stage is deliberate. Here's exactly what happens between the harvest and the moment you open the box.

Come see for yourself
01 — Sourcing

We go to the field

Nothing enters our kitchen without us meeting the person who grew it. We buy small, we buy seasonal, and we pay above market because good farming should be sustainable for the farmer too.

02 — Craft

Made in small batches

No industrial mixers, no shortcuts, no preservatives. Dough rests overnight. Fruit is folded in by hand. Jars are filled while the contents are still warm, and every batch is tasted before it leaves.

03 — Rest

Time does the work

Fermentation, infusion, cold-steeping — the stages where flavour actually develops. We don't rush them. A sourdough gets 24 hours. A truffle salt gets three weeks. A cold brew gets eighteen hours.

04 — Delivery

To your table, same day

Insulated, recyclable packaging keeps everything at the right temperature. Baked goods leave the oven before dawn and reach you before the day is out — never frozen, never warehoused.
